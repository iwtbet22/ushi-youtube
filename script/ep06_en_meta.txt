Do Japanese pro baseball teams really make money? We break down the finances of the six Central League clubs, through the businesses of the companies that own them.
The Yomiuri Giants and Chunichi Dragons don't publish their results. Meanwhile, the Hanshin Tigers made a net profit of 2.7 billion yen, and the Yokohama DeNA BayStars 2.4 billion yen.
How do newspapers like the Yomiuri and the Chunichi make money as circulation falls? What is Hankyu Hanshin's biggest earner? Why does Yakult sell more overseas than in Japan?
We compare parent company businesses, club results, attendance, payrolls (foreign players estimated), and who owns each ballpark.

What you'll learn
- Where a club's money comes from and goes: the Hiroshima Carp's 17.46 billion yen in revenue (tickets, merchandise and more)
- A 1954 tax rule: money a parent company spends to cover its club's losses counts as "advertising"
- Yomiuri: circulation down by half. Nippon TV, Yomiuriland (amusement park, horse racing facilities), Giants Town, and a stake in the Tokyo Dome
- Hankyu Hanshin Holdings: real estate is the top earner. The Tigers' pennant lifted its sports business to 57.1 billion yen
- DeNA: games, live streaming and sports. Why January to March loses money
- Chunichi Shimbun: the Tokyo Shimbun is also its paper. Revenue fell below 100 billion yen for the first time in 44 years, yet net profit was 5.9 billion yen
